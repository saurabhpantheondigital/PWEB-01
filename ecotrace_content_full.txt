SECTION 2  |  WHY EPR IS MANDATORY IN INDIA

— Section label —
Why Every Indian Company That Uses Plastic Packaging Must Act Now

The Law Has Changed. EPR Compliance Is No Longer Optional.
The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC) notified the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, 2022, making Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) legally mandatory for every Producer, Importer, and Brand Owner (PIBO) that introduces plastic packaging into the Indian market. Subsequent amendments in 2023, 2024, and 2026 have progressively tightened obligations, expanded the scope of covered entities, and increased recycling and recycled content targets year on year.
This is not a future requirement. It is in force right now. Every company — from a large FMCG corporation to a regional dairy brand to a D2C startup — that uses plastic packaging must register on CPCB's centralised EPR portal, calculate its annual plastic waste liability, procure recycling certificates, and file an annual return every year by 30 June.
Non-compliance is increasingly costly. CPCB has issued show-cause notices to hundreds of companies for FY 2022–23 and FY 2023–24. The agency publishes a public list of defaulters every year by 30 September — every company that missed its target or failed to file its annual return is named. Enforcement is no longer theoretical.

What the Law Now Requires — Five Parallel Obligations
	•	Registration: Register on CPCB's centralised EPR portal (eprplastic.cpcb.gov.in) before commencing operations. Companies in 3+ states register with CPCB. Companies in 1–2 states register with the relevant SPCB.
	•	Recycling targets: Meet annual category-wise recycling targets by procuring EPR certificates from CPCB-registered recyclers. Targets are increasing each FY and reach 100% by FY 2027–28 for most categories.
	•	Recycled content (URep) — new from FY 2025–26: Use a minimum percentage of recycled plastic in your own new packaging. Tracked separately via URep certificates. Targets increase through 2028–29.
	•	Reuse obligation — new from FY 2025–26: Category I (rigid) brand owners must meet reuse targets for containers above 0.9 litres/kg. Declared on CPCB portal annually.
	•	Annual return and quarterly data entry: File annual return by 30 June every year. Enter quarterly sales and procurement data with GST invoice numbers on CPCB portal throughout the year.

SECTION 5  |  CPCB AND SPCB — BOTH REGULATORY AUTHORITIES COVERED

— Section label —
Complete CPCB and SPCB Compliance — In One Platform

Understanding Your Regulatory Authority: CPCB vs SPCB
India's EPR framework divides regulatory authority between CPCB at the national level and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) at the state level. The rule is simple: if your company introduces plastic packaging in more than 2 states, you register and file with CPCB. If you operate in 1 or 2 states, you register with the relevant SPCB. EcoTrace supports both paths.

CPCB — Central Pollution Control Board
Portal: eprplastic.cpcb.gov.in · Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, New Delhi

Applies when: Your company introduces plastic packaging in MORE than 2 states or union territories

	•	Register on CPCB centralised portal — mandatory before operations. Unique 15-digit EPR registration number issued. Initial validity: 1 year. Renewal: 3 years.
	•	File Annual Return on CPCB portal by 30 June every year — plastic consumption, certificates, state-wise data, URep claims, audit status
	•	Enter quarterly sales and procurement transaction data on CPCB portal with GST invoice numbers throughout the year
	•	Maintain CPCB certificate wallet — all recycling and end-of-life certificates procured and transferred via portal
	•	Mandatory URep certificates from FY 2025–26 — generated by PIBO on portal, linked to recycled content used in own packaging
	•	Third-party audit by CPCB-empanelled auditor (IITs, NITs, CSIRs) — triggered based on certificate volumes
	•	Annual processing fee = 25% of registration fee — paid at time of annual return filing
	•	Environmental Compensation levied by CPCB directly for shortfall or non-filing via portal
	•	Registration renewal every 3 years with updated CTO/CTE consent numbers from SPCB

SPCB — State Pollution Control Board
Authority: Relevant state SPCB or Pollution Control Committee (PCC) — 28 SPCBs + 8 PCCs across India

Applies when: Your company introduces plastic packaging in 1 or 2 states or union territories only

	•	Register with the relevant SPCB or PCC through the centralised CPCB portal — same online system, SPCB is the approving authority
	•	Obtain valid Consent to Establish (CTE) and Consent to Operate (CTO) under the Air and Water Acts from SPCB — required before EPR registration. CPCB portal auto-fetches validity and blocks registration if consents are expired.
	•	File Annual Return via the same CPCB portal — SPCB receives and reviews your state-level data independently
	•	SPCB submits its own annual report to CPCB covering all PIBOs and PWPs in that state — your portal data feeds this directly
	•	SPCB can initiate independent compliance proceedings, surprise inspections, and separate EC notices — beyond any CPCB action
	•	Physical verification of recyclers conducted jointly by CPCB Regional Directorate and SPCB within 30 days of recycler registration
	•	SPCB / PCC is the final authority on registration cancellation or suspension for state-registered entities

Important: Even SPCB-registered companies file their annual return on the same CPCB portal (eprplastic.cpcb.gov.in). The difference is the approving authority and fee payment. EcoTrace supports both CPCB and SPCB filing workflows — same one-click annual return generation for all clients.

The Certificates Your Company Needs — And What Each One Means
Under India's EPR framework, companies fulfil their compliance obligations through distinct types of certificates — each serving a different purpose under the Plastic Waste Management Rules. EcoTrace manages all of them, tracking each certificate in your ledger with CPCB certificate IDs, recycler registration numbers, GST invoice numbers, and transfer dates.

#
Certificate Type
What It Is and How It Works
Key Rules (FY 2025–26)
1
Recycling Certificate
Issued by a CPCB-registered Plastic Waste Processor confirming a specific quantity of plastic packaging waste has been mechanically or chemically recycled. One certificate equals one MT of plastic processed. These are tradeable on CPCB's Electronic Trading Platform — companies with surplus can sell to those with shortfalls.
1 cert = 1 MT · Category-specific · Tradeable · Transferred via CPCB wallet · Certificates from unregistered recyclers are legally invalid
2
End-of-Life Certificate
Issued for plastic packaging that cannot be recycled and is processed through co-processing in cement or steel kilns, waste-to-energy, waste-to-oil, or road construction. The permissible share of EPR obligation that can be met through end-of-life disposal is capped and declining each financial year as CPCB drives toward higher recycling.
Declining EOL cap each FY · Primarily Cat III and IV · Co-processing, WtE, WtO, road construction · EOL redefined under 2026 amendment Rule 3(ga)
3
URep Certificate (Recycled Content)
The Use of Recycled Plastic (URep) certificate is generated by the PIBO itself — not by a recycler — to confirm that recycled plastic content has been used in their own new packaging. Mandatory from FY 2025–26 with minimum percentages per category increasing annually. Cat I: 30% in FY 25–26 rising to 60% from FY 28–29. Cat II: 10% from FY 25–26. Cat III: 5% from FY 25–26.
Self-generated by PIBO · Mandatory FY 25–26 onwards · Cat I: 30% → 60% by FY 28–29 · Cat II: 10% → 20% · Cat III: 5% → 10% · FSSAI/CDSCO food and pharma exemptions available
4
Reuse Certificate (Cat I)
Applicable to Category I rigid packaging only. If a company reuses its own rigid plastic packaging — for example refilling bottles or returning containers — the quantity reused is deducted from the eligible quantity calculation, reducing the EPR obligation for that year. Declared on CPCB portal annually. Reuse of food-contact packaging is subject to FSSAI regulations.
Cat I rigid only · Reduces eligible quantity · Containers 0.9–4.9L/kg: 10% target in FY 25–26 · Containers >4.9L/kg: 70% target in FY 25–26 · FSSAI rules apply
5
ULB / PRO Credit
Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) and Producer Responsibility Organisations (PROs) can generate and transfer EPR credits to PIBOs as part of city-level plastic waste collection and channelisation programs. This mechanism allows PIBOs to fulfil part of their obligation through formal waste collection systems rather than exclusively through recyclers.
Via registered ULBs or PROs · Quantity exchanged for EPR credits · Must be documented and submitted on CPCB portal at annual return

SECTION 9  |  THE COST OF NON-COMPLIANCE

— Section label —
CPCB is enforcing. The penalties are real. And they are growing every year.

What Happens When You Miss EPR Targets or Filing Deadlines
CPCB has issued show-cause notices to hundreds of PIBOs for non-compliance in FY 2022–23 and 2023–24. It publishes a public defaulters list every year by 30 September. After the annual return deadline of 30 June, CPCB auto-files the return and levies maximum Environmental Compensation automatically — there is no manual override and no grace period.

₹2,000 per MT shortfall
Environmental Compensation (EC) for every MT of EPR target not met at year-end. A company that is short by 1,000 MT pays ₹20,00,000 in EC. Shortfall carries forward for up to 3 years — at least one-third must be cleared per year.
₹5,000 per MT for non-filing
2.5× the standard EC rate applies if the annual return is not filed by 30 June. After the deadline, CPCB auto-files at maximum EC with no warning and no possibility of reversal.
1-year business ban
Submitting false or misleading data results in cancellation of EPR registration and a 1-year ban from operations. The company is blacklisted from future EPR registration. This applies even to accidental errors in submitted data.
Public defaulters list
CPCB publishes every non-compliant PIBO by name on its website by 30 September each year. This is a reputational risk — customers, investors, and partners can see your compliance status publicly.
SPCB independent action
State Pollution Control Boards have authority to initiate compliance proceedings, surprise inspections, and separate EC notices for violations in their state — independent of CPCB action nationally.
Shortfall carry-forward
Unfulfilled obligations do not disappear. They carry forward to the next year. After 3 years, full EC is levied on the original shortfall and cannot be refunded even if eventually fulfilled.
